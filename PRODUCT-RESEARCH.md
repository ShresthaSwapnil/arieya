# Arieya product research

Research reviewed 23 September 2026. Public studies guide the design; they do not establish Arieya’s impact. Measure the product with Aurora Jewel Studio and later pilot stores before publishing outcome claims.

## Shopper problems this product addresses

1. **People may not know the store’s product vocabulary.** A shopper can know the occasion, recipient, budget, or feeling without knowing which category or filter combination expresses it.
2. **Product lists can be hard to scan and narrow.** Baymard’s product-list study recruited shoppers aged 21–56 to test 19 e-commerce sites in eight categories. The study reports hundreds of usability issues related to product listing, filtering, and sorting. Its findings support clearer, relevant shortlists; they do not prove that chat improves conversion.
3. **Shoppers need useful, responsive advice to trust it.** A study of 173 Spanish online shoppers found perceived usefulness and responsiveness positively associated with trust in the seller. The sample skewed young: 56.1% were 18–25, so it is not evidence about all age groups.
4. **Older and disabled shoppers face interaction barriers that are often avoidable.** W3C guidance highlights needs around text readability and contrast, target size and motor precision, understandable controls, feedback, memory, and distraction. WCAG practices address many of these needs.
5. **Product truth matters.** A recommendation is only helpful when price, availability, materials, and other attributes match the merchant’s actual catalogue. Arieya should disclose when source data is missing or may be stale.

## What this means for the full-page advisor

- Use the full page for a deliberate guided-shopping task; make the invitation and purpose clear before the shopper enters.
- Keep an obvious way to leave, restart, or return to the store.
- Use plain language, large readable type, visible focus, keyboard support, accessible names, clear status updates, and a generous mobile composer.
- Show the product cards in the conversation, with visible price and a direct product-page action.
- Ask only useful follow-up questions. Offer prompt suggestions, but never require them.
- Say when there is no exact match. Do not fill the screen with irrelevant products.
- Test with real shoppers. Current studies do not establish that a full-page chat outperforms a product finder, search, or compact assistant for Arieya’s target stores.

## Research coverage and next study

“Every demographic” cannot be established from one public paper or one pilot. Include a mix of ages, gender identities, shopping confidence, disability and assistive-technology use, language, income and gifting experience. Avoid treating age as a proxy for ability.

Recruit at least 20 shoppers for moderated discovery and task testing, intentionally including participants aged 18–25, 26–40, 41–55, 56–69, and 70+. Include keyboard-only and screen-reader users, low-vision users, and shoppers with varied digital confidence. Ask participants to:

- Find a meaningful gift from a 30+ product catalogue using the current store experience.
- Repeat the same task with Arieya.
- Refine the recommendation after changing one constraint.
- Explain what information made the recommendation trustworthy or questionable.
- Complete the task on both a phone and a desktop where possible.

Record task completion, time to a relevant product, wrong turns, requests for help, trust and confidence feedback, and accessibility barriers. This is formative research, not a representative estimate of all shoppers.

## What to measure with Aurora Jewel Studio

| Measure | Definition | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Advisor start rate | Advisor starts ÷ eligible store visits | Whether shoppers notice and choose the experience |
| Recommendation rate | Conversations receiving at least one result ÷ conversations started | Whether catalogue and conversation work together |
| Product click rate | Unique product-link clicks ÷ unique recommendation impressions | Whether a recommendation earns a next step |
| Refinement rate | Conversations with a shopper follow-up ÷ conversations with a result | Whether shoppers can steer the shortlist |
| No-match rate | No-match results ÷ completed requests | Reveals catalogue gaps and unsupported requests |
| Pilot merchant retention | Pilot stores still actively using Arieya at days 30, 60, and 90 ÷ stores that began that cohort | Whether merchants continue after setup |
| Paid merchant retention | Paying stores retained at period end ÷ paying stores at period start, reported by cohort | Whether the paid product keeps delivering value |

Count a product impression only when its card becomes visible. Use a stable anonymous conversation ID, store ID, product ID, and timestamp. Deduplicate repeat events. Do not interpret product clicks as purchases or claim incremental sales without a suitable comparison. Before billing exists, track pilot activity and intent; do not label it paid retention.

## Founding-store offer to test

**A guided catalogue setup and branded advisor preview for an online store with 30+ products.** Arieya helps map the first catalogue, shapes the shopping conversation around the store, and reviews early shopper signals with the merchant. The merchant can evaluate the experience before agreeing to a paid subscription.

Keep the pilot scope clear: one catalogue, one website, a defined pilot period, named success measures, and a short review. Confirm any free setup commitment, capacity, data terms, and duration before accepting stores. The landing page currently invites merchants to request a conversation; it does not promise a fixed pilot length or subscription price.
